We swapped the contractor for AI, not the way we work

Companies used to delegate fixes and enhancements to pools of subcontracted developers. Now they delegate the same work to AI agents. The executor changed, and the way we work did not. The defenses we built around subcontracted developers failed, and they fail the same way with AI agents.

How a subcontracted pool works

For more than 15 years, as a tech lead and software engineer, I delegated fixes and enhancements to subcontracted companies. I defined each task and validated the delivery before it went to production and before the company paid for it.

The subcontracted company kept a pool of developers and assigned each task to whoever was available. Contracts had incentives to learn the product, but price usually weighed more, so the assigned developer did not always know it.

The task described a symptom or a small change. In theory, that was enough to close it. In practice, the missing knowledge and context led to technical debt.

This contradiction always bothered me. The person who knew the product did not change the code, and the people who changed the code did not know the product.

AI agents work the same way

Today an engineer who delegates to an AI agent holds the role I held.

Subcontracted poolAI agent
The tech lead knows the product, defines the task and accepts the deliveryThe engineer knows the product, writes the prompt and reviews the generated code
The subcontracted company keeps the pool and assigns whoever is availableThe AI tool starts a new session for each request
A pool developer executes without knowing the productAn agent session executes without knowing the product
A ticket carries a partial specA prompt or an issue carries a partial spec
The goal is to close the ticketThe goal is to fulfill the request
The company pays by an effort metricThe company pays by tokens, which reflect effort

The engineer translates a request for someone else to execute and answers for a delivery they did not make.

Why the defenses failed

We knew the risks of this arrangement and built defenses against them. In my experience, all of them failed in practice. I have also seen each failure with AI agents.

DefenseSubcontracted poolWith AI agents
Specs and acceptance criteriaNever covered every behaviorNever cover every behavior
Review of every deliveryToo much volume for one personEven more volume
Automated testsFew, and written by the subcontracted company. They proved that the code did what it implemented, not what it should doWritten by the agent for its own code, with the same flaw
DocumentationWritten by the subcontracted companyWritten by the agent
Keeping contextThe people who knew the product left or forgotAGENTS.md holds only the essentials

Specs also protected the contract. With a written acceptance criterion, I could demand a fix under the warranty, at the subcontracted company’s cost. An AI agent offers no warranty. When its work is wrong, we spend tokens to redo it.

Two causes explain the five failures.

Whoever executes also verifies

Review was the only independent check, and the volume of deliveries already made it impossible. Agents make the volume larger.

The tests and the documentation came from the same company as the code. A different developer wrote them, and sometimes a different company, but each one worked from the previous delivery and none of them knew the product. A misunderstanding in the code passed into the tests and the documentation, and nothing caught it.

Agents repeat this. A separate session or a different agent receives the code with the misunderstanding already in it.

Product knowledge does not fit in a document

Where the spec was silent, the developer assumed and did not ask, because asking was not their responsibility. Some assumptions were wrong. I have seen deliveries that had nothing to do with the task. An agent also assumes without asking, and we call its wrong result a hallucination.

A longer spec did not help. The more detail we wrote, the more the developers forgot or confused. An agent also loses track of detailed documentation. A 2023 study, Lost in the Middle, found that language models use the start and the end of a long context better than the middle.

The people who knew the product could not fill the gaps either. They left, were busy with other work, or could not remember everything. An AGENTS.md file has the same limit. It holds the essentials, not the whole application.

Same way of working, same problems

Neither cause depends on who executes the work. When we replaced the subcontracted company with an AI tool, we kept the three roles, the partial specs, the reviews nobody can finish, the tests written by the executor and the knowledge that no document holds. So we kept the problems too.

Did we gain or lose? We gained speed and lowered the cost. We will not gain anything else while we keep working the same way. The advantages of AI over a subcontractor are the subject of the next article in this series.
